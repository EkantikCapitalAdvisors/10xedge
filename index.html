HONEST ODDS

10× is the design ceiling, not the expectation.

We consider reaching it unlikely, and we say so before we start. The experiment is built so that each of its three possible endings shows something.

The three ways the experiment can end, and what each one shows.
OutcomeWhat it shows
The account reaches 10× its start The design can hit its ceiling under live conditions.
The account ends between the floor and 10× How the edge behaves over a full year, with every trade on record.
The account stops at the pre-set floor The risk rules work as written.

How close the floor is.From $3,000, four maximum-size losses in a row reach the floor. That is arithmetic from the rules below, not a forecast of what will happen.

The only failed experiment is one where a rule is broken.

WHY OPTIONS

A known loss. An open-ended gain.

A long option costs its premium and can lose no more than that. If price travels through the strike before expiry, the payoff steepens. That shape is why Ascent trades options — and why the account can fall quickly.

WHY IT CAN WORK

The risk is known to the dollar at entry: the premium. The reward depends on how far price moves before expiry. One winner can pay for several losers — if the winners come.

WHY IT FAILS

Short-dated options are brittle. In a choppy market the premium decays faster than price moves, and losses arrive in clusters. The rules react to realised losses; they do not try to predict the market.

THE RULES

Fixed before the first trade.

These are the whole rulebook. They do not change while the experiment runs, and the same rules apply on a winning week as on a losing one.

The rules of the experiment.
RuleValue
Starting capital $3,000, the founder’s own money. No additions, no withdrawals.
Start date 1 December 2026
End date 30 November 2027
Instruments Long options only: SPX contracts that expire the same day, and single-stock options 7–10 days from expiry.
Maximum risk per trade 10% of the account ($300 at the start)
Maximum total open risk 10% of the account
Position sizing One contract per $3,000 of account equity. Minimum account $3,000.
Rule changes None mid-experiment. Any amendment is published and countersigned before it takes effect.

HOW SIZE STEPS UP

Position size steps up by one contract for every $3,000 of account equity, and steps back down the same way. The account trades one contract until equity reaches $6,000. Because size follows equity, the target is a multiple, not a dollar figure: the same rules run at any account size from the minimum.

Charter dated 9 November 2026. These rules are published here before the first trade on 1 December 2026.

KILL CRITERIA

Three things end it early.

The experiment stops if any one of these happens. Each is published in advance, and none of them asks for an opinion.

  1. 1

    Floor

    Account equity closes at or below $2,000. Trading stops. A final report follows within 7 days.

  2. 2

    Rule breach

    Any trade outside the rules above. The experiment ends and is reported as a breach, not a result.

  3. 3

    Edge check

    Expectancy over the last 30 trades turns negative. Trading pauses until the witness reviews it.

If none of these happens, the experiment ends on 30 November 2027.

Read that demo average with its error bars: on 37 alerts with a logged risk it sits somewhere between +0.15R and +0.84R at 95% confidence.

These demo alerts were not sized under the experiment’s rules. 17 of the 37 with a logged risk put more than $300 at risk — above the 10% limit on a $3,000 account. The live account cannot take those trades at that size.

Thirty-nine demo trades is not proof. It is the reason to run the experiment with real money, in the open.
